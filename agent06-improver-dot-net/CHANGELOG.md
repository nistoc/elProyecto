# Changelog

All notable changes to the agent06-improver-dot-net (TranslationImprover) project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **A run about the words in doubt (`candidates_only`).** New request flag (`SubmitRefineJobRequest.candidates_only`, proto field 19; REST `candidatesOnly`). No line goes to the model to be rewritten: code finds the words that may be Spanish written in Cyrillic or that mix letters of both alphabets (`SpellingCandidates`, now also short words inside Spanish speech), the model says for each in which language it was said (`ISpellingJudge`, `OpenAISpellingJudge`: ten words to a request, JSON answer, the word repeated so that an answer of another item is dropped), and code writes the spelling the transcript already has (`CandidateRespelling`). A word of both alphabets is brought to one alphabet in either direction («Теngo» → "Tengo", «Nет» → «Нет») when few letters are in question and each has one reading. Default model of such a run: `gpt-4.1-mini`. The run record and the block files show the words asked about (`askedWords`, proto `RefineBatchDelta.asked_words`), the words written anew and the reasons the others stayed. Measured on 33 lesson dialogues: 54 words written anew — 43 right, 2 harmful, 9 doubtful — in 60 requests (about $0.03 for all 33); the run that sends every line with the check of edits kept 15 changes (13 right) in about 2,840 requests. See README, "Words in doubt". Tests: `CandidateRespellingTests`, `OpenAISpellingJudgeTests`, `RefinePipelineWordsInDoubtTests`.

### Fixed

- **The check of edits no longer turns a Russian word into a Spanish one because it sounds like it.** A word in Cyrillic letters respelled by the model was kept whenever it sounded like a Spanish word of the transcript — and «институт», «история», «момент» do. On 33 lesson dialogues 7 of the 24 changes kept were such words. `RespellingGuard` now asks for Spanish next to the word: the nearest word on either side in Latin letters, or another word it respells (a phrase in Cyrillic carries itself). Not asked of a word of both alphabets, of a word that is its whole line, and of a transcript that writes its Spanish in Cyrillic as a habit — the run reads that off the transcript (`SpellingCandidates.Habit`: from 10 % of its Spanish words, at least 20 of them). Replayed over the stored answers of the model: lesson dialogues 24 kept (14 right, 7 harmful, 3 doubtful) → 15 kept (13 right, 1 harmful, 1 doubtful); 3 transcripts of the older transcriber 71 → 75, all right. Cost: a Spanish word quoted alone in Russian speech stays in Cyrillic (3 such in the lesson set).
- **Sound of «лл» and «ль».** A doubled Cyrillic «л» was read as the Spanish "ll" («коллега» did not sound like "colega"); «ль» before a vowel was read as "li" only, so «бомбилья» scored 0.75 against "bombilla". Both are fixed (`RespellingGuard.SoundForm`, `Likeness`).
- **A run says what went wrong and what it cost.** A block whose request failed kept its lines and the job read `Completed` with nothing but a log line to tell; the tokens billed were not read at all. The job status (REST and gRPC: `failed_batches`, `prompt_tokens`, `completion_tokens`, `edits_let_through`, `edits_put_back`), the run record (new section `## Outcome`) and the callback (`failedBatches`) now carry them. An answer of the API without text is a failed block (it used to pass as "answered, nothing changed"). A run that cannot read its transcript ends `Failed` (the job stayed `Pending` for ever).
- **The output file has the form of its source.** The refined file was always written with a byte order mark, the machine's line ends and one empty line more than the source had. It now keeps the source's mark, line ends and last line end; a run that changes nothing gives the source back byte for byte (`TranscriptTextTests`).
- **REST submit keeps the check of edits.** `POST /api/refine/jobs` copies the request field by field, and `respellingOnly`, `systemPrompt` and `sourceName` were left out of that copy: a REST run asked with `respellingOnly: true` took the model's lines as they were and its run record said "Check of edits: off", with no error. The gRPC entry (what agent05 calls) was not affected. The three fields are now passed on; `RefineControllerSubmitTests` holds it.

### Changed

- **The run about the words in doubt puts every request to two models.** One answer of the judge is one draw: the same 33 lesson dialogues, run a second time with the same prompt, model and temperature 0, got another language for 12 of the 451 words, and five of those were Russian words now called Spanish (65 words written anew against 54; 7 harmful against 2). First tried: the same request made twice, a word written anew only when the answers agree. Replayed over the stored answers of two runs it promised 2 harmful words; measured live it wrote 62 words, 6 of them harmful — two requests made one after the other agree also where they are wrong — and it was dropped. Now a request goes to the model of the request (`gpt-4.1-mini` by default) and to `gpt-4.1` (`RefineJobRequestMapper.JudgesOf`, `SecondJudgeModel`), and a word is written anew only when both name the same language (`CandidateRespelling.Agreed`); a word they differ on stays, counted in the run record as "the models do not name the same language". The two err on different words: asked at the same hour, the small model called 6 Russian words Spanish and the larger one 4, one word in common. A block carries the answer of each model in `rawText` under its name (`[gpt-4.1-mini]`, `[gpt-4.1]`) and the tokens of both; the record says "Models: … and …", "requests: N, each put to 2 models" and the tokens of each model apart. Measured through the REST entry on the 33 dialogues: 55 words written anew — 45 right, none harmful, 10 doubtful; $0.20 against $0.03 for one model. Tests: `CandidateRespellingTests`, `RefinePipelineWordsInDoubtTests`, `RefineJobRequestMapperTests`.
- **A word the teacher names is written also when only `gpt-4.1` calls it Spanish.** The rule of two models held back right words that `gpt-4.1` alone found (9 on the 33 lesson dialogues), and those are lesson vocabulary. Now a word in Cyrillic with exactly the sound of a Spanish word of the transcript, which `gpt-4.1` calls Spanish and the other model does not, is written when that Spanish word stands in Latin letters in the same line or in the line before or after it (`CandidateRespelling.NamedNextTo`; the lines as they were before the run). Not for a short word, not for a word of both alphabets, not for a word only the small model calls Spanish (of its 5 such words 2 were Russian). The run record marks a word written this way and names the rule in its "Mode" line. Counted on two stored sets of answers: 6 and 5 words more, every one right; not run live. Tests: `CandidateRespellingTests`, `RefinePipelineWordsInDoubtTests`.
- **One way in for a refine request, and a run in parts.** REST and gRPC used to copy and check the request each on its own (a field added to one copy was lost in the other without an error). Both now call `RefineJobSubmitter`; `RefineJobRequestMapper` trims the request, gives the defaults and checks the paths. `RefineJobRequestMapperTests` fills every property of the request and every field of the proto message by reflection, so a field forgotten in the mapper fails a test. `RefinePipeline.RunAsync` was one method doing everything; it is now the plan of the run (`PlanAsync`), the blocks (`RefineBlocksAsync` / `RefineBlockAsync`), and three helpers — `TranscriptText` (reading and writing the transcript), `RefineRunRecord` (`*.prompt.md`), `RefineBatchFiles` (`batch_*.json`). The HTTP call to the model is `OpenAIChatClient`; `OpenAIRefineClient` builds the prompt of a block and reads its lines out of the answer (`OpenAIAnswerTests`). Block records gain `rawText` (the model's answer before the check), the counts of the check and the tokens.
- **Built-in refine prompt reworded as copying with one exception.** The built-in user template (`FilePromptLoader.DefaultPrompt`) and the system prompt (`RefinePipeline.SystemPrompt`) no longer say that the transcript "contains Russian speech where Spanish was transcribed in Cyrillic" and no longer give a numbered task to find and replace. They say: copy the lines of the batch; the transcript is bilingual on purpose, keep every word in the language and alphabet it has, the speaker's mistakes included; one kind of word is respelled — a Spanish word written in Cyrillic by ear — and such words are rare. Reason: transcripts made by `gpt-transcribe` are already right in most lines, and the old wording made `gpt-4o-mini` look for things to fix. Measured on one lesson dialogue of 593 lines (batch 5, context 2, temperature 0): the old wording changed 29 lines — about 3 for the better, about 13 Russian words translated or broken into mixed script, about 9 "corrections" of the speaker's own Spanish; a wording with a list of "never" rules and an example of each changed 47 lines (the model did the very things the examples named: Russian words written in Latin letters, whole Russian lines translated); the wording kept here changed 7 lines — 1 for the better, 6 small ones for the worse. So the refiner is now close to doing nothing on such a transcript, and its output still has to be read. A prompt saved by the operator in agent05 (Refiner tab) is used instead of the built-in one, as before. Tests: `BuiltInPromptTests`.
- **Built-in refine prompt:** the line-format instruction no longer names `speaker_N` only — it reads `- TIME SPEAKER: "text"` and says the label may be `speaker_0` or a participant name and must not be changed. Needed for dialogue transcripts merged from participant tracks (agent05 `POST /api/jobs/dialogue`), whose lines carry names. Not yet checked against the live model.

### Added

- **Short words in Spanish speech.** The check of edits put back every word shorter than three sounds. A word of one or two sounds («ла», «эль», «йо», «ва») is now respelled when its sound is exactly that of the new word, Spanish stands on every side it has a neighbour on, and it is not a Russian word (a list of them in `RespellingGuard`: «и», «а», «но», «да», «мы», «ты»…).
- **The run record lists what the check let through.** Section `## Check of edits` of `*.prompt.md` names the rule about the neighbours that was in force (and the two counts it was chosen by) and every change kept, with the time mark of its line.
- **The Spanish of a transcript and the words in doubt, as code.** `SpanishLexicon` (the words in Latin letters as the transcript spells them, looked up by sound), `SpellingCandidates` (words of both alphabets; words in Cyrillic that sound like a word of the lexicon), `TranscriptWords` (tokens and alphabets of a line).
- **Check of edits (`respelling_only`).** New request flag (`SubmitRefineJobRequest.respelling_only`, proto field 18; REST `respellingOnly`): of what the model changed in a block `RespellingGuard` keeps only a word in Cyrillic letters respelled in Latin letters — the two must sound alike (0.85 on `SoundForm`), the new word must already occur in the transcript in Latin letters, digits and punctuation must stay — and puts the rest of the line back. The time mark and the speaker are never taken from the model; a block with another number of lines is put back whole; the next block gets the checked lines as context. The run record names the check and counts the changes let through and put back. Without the flag a run is what it was. On three stored answers for one dialogue (593 lines) the model changed 29, 47 and 7 lines; with the check changes stay in 3, 4 and 1, all of them respellings. See README, "Check of edits".
- **Per-job artifact root (refine):** **`SubmitRefineJobRequest.job_directory_relative`** — один сегмент под **`workspace_root`**; ввод/вывод и промежуточные файлы разрешаются от **`effectiveArtifactRoot`** = `workspace_root/job_directory_relative` при непустом поле; **`FilePromptLoader`** по-прежнему от глобального **`workspace_root`**. Тесты: **`RefineWorkspacePathsTests`**.
